### Uncovering complex evolutionary histories of somatic mutations

I lead the development and application of novel statistical methods aimed at uncovering complex evolutionary histories among somatic mutations that violate the *infinite sites model of evolution*, a simplifying assumption that asserts each mutation occurs only once within the phylogenetic history of a populations. By eliminating this assumption using a Bayesian approach for ancestral state reconstruction (called *stochastic character mapping*), we uncover previously hidden evolutionary complexity previously obscured by the infinite sites model, including revision and parallel mutation.

### Examining the role of telomere biology on hematopoiesis

I am interested in understanding the evolutionary mechanisms through which heritable telomere length influences health and disease. My work in this field focuses on applying the principles of phylogenetics, population genetics, and somatic evolution to examine the role of telomere shortening (and heritable telomere length) in governing hematopoiesis and leukemogenesis. Our research group has shown that heritable long telomere length promotes cancer risk by increasing the proliferative lifetime of progenitors, allowing cancer driver mutations acquired early in life to propagate into overt malignancies ([DeBoy & Tassia et al. 2023, *NEJM*](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2300503)).

### Studying evolution across multiple scales of time

I am fascinated by the processes through which evolution manifests trait variation across multiple scales of time. In addition to my focal work studying the processes and outcomes of evolution within a single individual's lifetime, I have also contributed to evolutionary studies at the scale of populations (e.g., [Taylor et al. 2024, *Nature*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07708-2)) and species (e.g., [Makova et al. 2024, *Nature*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07473-2)).
