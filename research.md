## Uncovering complex evolutionary histories of somatic mutations

Phylogenetic inference of single-cell-derived progenitor lineages has provided unprecedented insights into the origin and effects of somatic mutations across human tissues. Whether placed into the context of normal tissue development (e.g., aging$^1$) or disease (e.g., cancer$^2$), phylogeny uniquely empowers association between discrete somatic mutations and evolutionary traits (e.g., clonal fitness) that are obscured by bulk sampling.

I lead the development and application of novel statistical methods aimed at uncovering complex evolutionary histories among somatic mutations that violate the *infinite sites model of evolution*, a simplifying assumption that asserts each mutation occurs only once within the phylogenetic history of a sample. By eliminating this assumption using a Bayesian approach for ancestral state reconstruction (called *stochastic character mapping*), we uncover previously hidden evolutionary complexity previously obscured by the infinite sites model, including revision and parallel mutation. 

We are currently applying this method to understand the role of complex evolutionary histories throughout lifetimes of hematopoiesis.

*References:*  
$^1$Mitchell E, et al. Clonal dynamics of haematopoiesis across the human lifespan. *Nature* 606, 343-350 (2022). [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04786-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04786-y)  
$^2$Williams N, et al. Life histories of myeloproliferative neoplasms inferred from phylogenies. *Nature* 602, 162-168 (2022). [https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04312-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-04312-6)
